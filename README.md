## Case study 1: $37 per enterprise lead on the top LinkedIn campaigns, and the 10x creative gap behind it

**Headline results:** $37.11 cost per lead across the top three campaigns (67 leads), $34.94 on the best UK campaign, and 125 leads in total, with 69.6% of clicks from founders, CXOs, directors and VPs.

**Framing:** Consulting project

| | |
|---|---|
| Client | Banking technology company (name withheld) |
| Industry | Banking and fintech technology (B2B) |
| My role | Strategy, setup, targeting, optimisation and reporting |
| Channel | LinkedIn Ads |
| Markets | UK, Middle East, India |
| Formats | Single image, document, carousel, video |
| Total spend | $15,947.71 (USD) |
| Period | June to November 2025 |

### Goal

The account had two jobs. Lead generation campaigns offered a gated whitepaper to decision-makers in the UK and the Middle East. Awareness campaigns supported a UK expansion message, an AI Day event in India and a transaction banking proposition.

The business aim was to warm up a cold audience and hand the sales team leads they could reach out to.

### Strategy

- Campaigns were split by market and by objective, so every budget line had one job and one KPI to be judged on.
- A gated whitepaper was the lead offer, captured through lead forms, because it gives senior buyers a reason to share their details before they are ready for a sales call.
- The same offer ran in different formats and creative variants, so format and creative could be compared on cost per lead rather than on clicks.
- Awareness ran in parallel with image, carousel and video to warm up the UK and India markets.

I chose a whitepaper because the product, AI for banking, involves complex decision-making. A cold audience wants to study the subject before talking to anyone, so a download in exchange for their details was the right first step. It also warmed the audience up for the sales team to reach out.

### Execution

The account ran 14 campaigns and 10 tracked creatives across three markets. Lead generation took 54% of spend ($8,635.96) and awareness took 43% ($6,824.59). About $487 sat in campaigns outside the detailed breakdown.

I used manual bidding and reviewed it every week, adjusting bids based on reach and CPM.

### Analysis

#### Cost per lead by lead generation campaign

| Campaign | Spend | Clicks | Leads | CTR | Click to lead | CPL |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Middle East, document | $310.00 | 75 | 14 | 0.53% | 18.7% | **$22.14** |
| UK, image variant 2 | $1,502.61 | 209 | 43 | 0.72% | 20.6% | **$34.94** |
| UK, image variant 1 | $674.00 | 51 | 10 | 0.61% | 19.6% | $67.40 |
| Middle East, image | $2,258.29 | 220 | 31 | 0.67% | 14.1% | $72.85 |
| UK, document | $3,529.69 | 184 | 26 | 0.37% | 14.1% | $135.76 |
| UK, image variant 3 | $361.37 | 32 | 1 | 0.55% | 3.1% | $361.37 |

#### What the data showed

- **The top three campaigns averaged $37.11 per lead.** The Middle East document ad and the two strongest UK image variants produced 67 leads on $2,486.61, more than half of all leads on 29% of lead generation spend.
- **Creative was the biggest lever.** Two UK image variants promoting the same whitepaper landed at $34.94 and $361.37 per lead, a gap of more than 10x. The best variant also turned 20.6% of clicks into leads.
- **The best format depended on the market.** In the UK, the three image campaigns averaged $47.00 per lead against $135.76 for the document ad. In the Middle East it was the other way round, with the document ad at $22.14 against $72.85 for image, although on a smaller $310 budget.
- **One creative carried the account.** The best whitepaper creative produced 52 of the 125 leads (42%) at about $45.82 per lead.
- **By market,** Middle East lead generation averaged $57.07 per lead and UK lead generation averaged $75.85.
- **The right people were clicking.** Founders, CEOs, managing directors, directors, VPs, board members and executive directors made up 69.6% of clicks. Named banks such as ICICI Bank, HSBC, Lloyds Banking, Barclays and JPMorgan appeared among the top companies reached, and London was the largest location by impressions.
- **Some audiences need a quality check.** The IT services segment clicked at 1.84%, roughly three times the other segments, but those users may be vendors rather than buyers. Education and real estate segments also received impressions, which points to targeting that could be tightened.
- **Small samples were treated with caution.** One India awareness campaign showed a 9.5% CTR, but on only 1,857 impressions, so it was not used to draw conclusions.

### Results

| Metric | Lead generation | Awareness | Whole account |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Spend (USD) | $8,635.96 | $6,824.59 | $15,947.71 |
| Impressions | 140,029 | 383,755 | 533,868 |
| Clicks | 771 | 1,448 | 2,336 |
| CTR | 0.55% | 0.38% | 0.44% |
| CPM | $61.67 | $17.78 | |
| Leads | 125 | 0 | 125 |
| Click to lead rate | 16.2% | n/a | 5.35% |
| Cost per lead | **$69.09** | n/a | $127.58 |

Across all lead generation campaigns the cost per lead was $69.09, which is the honest blended figure for an audience of senior banking decision-makers. All KPIs are recalculated from raw spend, impressions, clicks and leads.

There was no earlier campaign to compare against, because no LinkedIn campaigns had run before mine. The sales cycle is usually one to two years, so pipeline and revenue from these leads cannot be measured yet.

### Learnings

- In B2B LinkedIn, creative testing moves cost per lead far more than bid tweaks, so I would budget for a new variant every few weeks.
- Format results do not travel between markets, so each market needs its own image versus document test.
- A high CTR from an adjacent industry is not a win until lead quality confirms it.
- Awareness and lead generation should be reported separately, because blending them makes the cost per lead look almost twice as high as it was.

**What happened next:** The client first wanted to target only large banks. Based on the results, we decided to include mid-size and smaller banks too, and shifted 40% of the budget to them.

## Case study 2: 10,704 leads from a business software conference, and why the cheapest registration was not the cheapest lead

**Framing:** In-house at a global business software company

| | |
|---|---|
| Industry | Business software (ERP), B2B events |
| My role | End to end, from strategy to execution |
| Channels | Meta Ads, LinkedIn Ads (message ads and video) |
| Event | 11 to 12 September 2026, Gandhinagar, India |
| Total spend | $133,701 |
| Registrations | 22,289 (platform reported) |
| Leads | 10,704 |
| Main event turn-up | 12% |

### Goal

The goal was to fill an in-person conference and 11 industry tracks (accounting, HR, developers, AI, manufacturing, logistics, e-commerce, retail and POS, food and beverage, services and marketing) with registered business attendees, and to turn those registrations into sales leads.

### Strategy

- The campaign ran in two layers. A core layer promoted the whole event, segmented by language and city. An industry layer gave every track its own ad set and its own message.
- Meta carried reach and volume at a low CPM. LinkedIn carried precision through message ads sent to professionals by role and location.
- Regional language ads in Gujarati, Marathi and Tamil were tested against English to see whether local language lowered the cost of a registration.

The target audience was business owners across every track.

### Execution

The campaign ran 17 Meta ad sets and 16 LinkedIn ad sets. Meta optimised for completed registrations on the event website. On LinkedIn, message ads ran on maximum delivery and video ads on manual bids. Meta took 68% of spend ($90,602) and LinkedIn took 32% ($43,100). The Marathi and Tamil ad sets were switched off after spending $849.57 between them.

### Analysis

**Meta main event, by language and region**

| Ad set | Spend | Registrations | Cost per registration |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Gujarati | $1,052 | 298 | **$3.53** |
| English, core audience | $21,052 | 4,990 | $4.22 |
| English, India-wide | $15,789 | 3,250 | $4.86 |
| Marathi | $658.24 | 53 | $12.42 |
| Tamil | $191.33 | 8 | $23.92 |

**Industry tracks, from spend to leads**

| Industry track | Ad spend | Leads | Cost per lead |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| HR | $5,728 | 532 | **$10.77** |
| E-commerce | $6,004 | 422 | $14.23 |
| Food and beverage | $5,262 | 368 | $14.29 |
| Logistics | $5,569 | 326 | $17.08 |
| AI | $3,961 | 227 | $17.45 |
| Manufacturing | $7,027 | 367 | $19.15 |
| Accounting | $6,876 | 335 | $20.53 |
| Developers | $7,197 | 308 | $23.37 |
| Marketing | $7,374 | 305 | $24.18 |
| Services | $3,487 | 102 | $34.19 |
| Retail and POS | $5,789 | 165 | $35.08 |

**What the data showed**

- **Local language and local geography won on registrations.** Gujarati ads cost $3.53 per registration against $23.92 for Tamil, a 6.8x gap, for an event held in Gujarat. On LinkedIn, the Gujarat message campaign produced 85% of core event conversions, while Bangalore spent $192.89 for none.
- **LinkedIn message ads beat LinkedIn video.** Gujarat video cost $47.63 per conversion against $16.25 for message ads, and its CPM of $10.25 was 14 times Meta's $0.73.
- **The best registration channel depended on the audience.** LinkedIn was cheaper for job-title audiences such as HR (2.5x) and accounting (2.0x). Meta was cheaper for sector audiences such as e-commerce (26.6x) and logistics (10.2x).
- **Cheap registrations did not always become cheap leads.** Retail and POS had the cheapest registration in the whole campaign at $1.71, yet it had the most expensive leads at $35.08 each. Developers registered cheaply on LinkedIn but cost $23.37 per lead. HR was efficient at both stages, at $10.77 per lead.

### Results

| Layer | Ad spend | Registrations | Cost per registration | Leads | Cost per lead |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Main event | $69,427 | 10,110 | $6.86 | 7,247 | **$9.58** |
| Industry tracks | $64,275 | 12,179 | $5.27 | 3,457 | $18.59 |
| **Total** | **$133,701** | **22,289** | **$6.00** | **10,704** | **$12.49** |

The main event had a 12% turn-up rate. Registrations are platform reported, and one person may have registered through both platforms. Leads come only from paid campaigns and are counted in the event CRM. Cost per lead divides ad spend by those leads. Amounts are converted from Indian rupees at about 95 rupees to the US dollar, the rate during the campaign.

### Learnings

- For an in-person event, budget should start close to the venue and in the local language, then move outward only while it stays efficient.
- The channel should be chosen per audience. Job-title audiences belong on LinkedIn, and sector or owner audiences belong on Meta.
- Registration cost is the wrong final metric. Next time I would judge each track on cost per lead, so a cheap but low-intent audience like retail and POS cannot pull budget away from HR.
- A 12% turn-up rate means most of the value sits after registration, so reminder sequences and pre-event engagement deserve as much planning as the ads.

## Case study 3: 84,358 search leads in India at $3.11 each

**Framing:** In-house at a global business software company, with the paid search team

| | |
|---|---|
| Industry | Business software (ERP), B2B SaaS |
| My role | Looked after the India search campaigns with the team, including bid and keyword adjustments |
| Channel | Google Ads, search |
| Market | India |
| Spend | $262,001 |
| Period | 1 January to 25 November 2026 |
| Leads | 84,358 |
| Bidding | Target CPA |

### Goal

The India account runs one search campaign per product (CRM, manufacturing, ERP, inventory and others). The goal was a steady flow of leads at a cost that made sense for the Indian market, where only a small share of leads go on to buy.

### Approach

- Each product had its own campaign, keywords and ads, so the search term, the ad and the landing page matched.
- An industry campaign added 54 ad groups (hotels, construction, accounting firms, solar and more), so each sector saw an ad written for its business.
- Keywords moved from broad match to phrase match after broad match brought in conversions that were not relevant.
- Bids used Target CPA, with targets set from what a lead is worth in India rather than copied from other countries.

### Results

| Metric | India account |
|---|---:|
| Spend | $262,001 |
| Impressions | 5,859,761 |
| Clicks | 407,521 |
| CTR | 6.95% |
| Average CPC | $0.64 |
| Leads | 84,358 |
| Click to lead rate | 20.70% |
| Cost per lead | **$3.11** |

**Leads by campaign**

| Campaign | Leads | Cost per lead |
|---|---:|---:|
| CRM | 16,974 | $3.07 |
| Manufacturing (MRP) | 15,361 | $3.75 |
| ERP | 14,108 | $4.15 |
| Inventory | 10,020 | $2.34 |
| Purchase | 7,455 | $3.07 |
| Industry (54 ad groups) | 5,898 | $5.39 |
| Sales | 4,985 | $1.26 |

### What the data showed

- **Lead forms were the cheapest source of leads.** Lead form extensions produced 20,455 leads at $2.12 each with a 43.4% conversion rate, against $3.38 per lead from headline clicks.
- **Product intent beat broad interest.** Inventory came in at $2.34 per lead and CRM and Purchase at $3.07, while the wider ERP campaign cost $4.15.
- **Industry ad groups cost more but reached new sectors.** The industry campaign averaged $5.39 per lead. The strongest sectors are below.

| Construction | 592 | $5.38 | 10.9% |
| Fine dining | 548 | $6.02 | 11.1% |
| Real estate | 372 | $5.67 | 12.5% |
| Solar | 372 | $4.88 | 15.7% |
| Photography | 275 | $5.03 | 15.0% |
| Clothing | 208 | $4.89 | 16.8% |
| Fitness | 179 | $5.74 | 7.3% |
| Fast food | 173 | $4.86 | 13.1% |

Figures from Google Ads for India only. Other countries run with their own targets, so they are not combined here. Amounts are converted from euros at about $1.16 to the euro.
